## What changes the price: size and tread

Size is the first cut. Read the sidewall of the tire that is on the car now, or the placard in the door jamb if you do not trust the old tire. Load rating matters too. A tire that is the right diameter and the wrong load is not a discount. It is the wrong tire.

Tread is the second cut. Two used tires of the same size can be miles apart in life left. We look across the tread, not at one pretty groove. A tire that is already feathered on one shoulder may be cheap and still be a bad buy, because you will feel the old wear the whole time you own it. That pattern is often an alignment problem, which a cheap casing will not fix. The [alignment rack](/en/services/wheel-alignment) is in the same shop if the car is pulling.

We also look at the sidewalls. Cracks, a bubble, or a repair in the shoulder take the tire off the table no matter what someone hoped to pay. Florida heat finishes rubber that was already tired. A casing that looks fine in the shade can show those cracks once you turn it in the light. That inspection is part of the visit. It is described on the [used tires page](/en/services/used-tires).
