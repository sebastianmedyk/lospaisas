A tire can have plenty of tread and still be too old. In South Florida, heat and strong sun age rubber faster. The good news: every tire tells you when it was made. Here is how to read it, from **Los Paisas Tires Shop** in West Palm Beach.

## Where is the DOT date?

On the sidewall, find the letters **DOT** followed by a code. The **last four digits** are the date: the first two are the **week**, the last two are the **year**. Example: **2321** means the 23rd week of 2021. The full code is sometimes printed only on one side of the tire.

## Why age matters in Florida

Rubber dries out over time. Heat, UV light, and long periods parked in the sun speed that up. Signs of an aging tire:

- Small cracks in the sidewall or between tread blocks
- Rubber that looks dull, gray, or hard
- Bulges or a tread that feels uneven

Many tire and vehicle makers recommend a close inspection once a tire is about 5 years old and replacing it by around 6 to 10 years, even if the tread looks fine. Check your vehicle manual for your maker's advice.
