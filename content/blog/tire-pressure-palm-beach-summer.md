## Why summer air pressure changes in the car

Air expands as it warms. A tire set in the cool morning will read higher after a drive on hot pavement. That rise is expected. It is not, by itself, a reason to let air out in a gas-station lot at 3 p.m. If you bleed a hot tire down to the cold placard number, you will be underinflated the next morning, and an underinflated tire flexes, builds more heat, and wears the shoulders.

The opposite mistake is ignoring a tire that is low before you ever drive. Heat will not "fill it in." A tire that starts low stays the most stressed tire on the car. In West Palm Beach that shows up as a soft feel, a shoulder that wears, or a flat you discover when you need the car.

Check pressure cold, before a long drive, with a gauge you trust more than the sticker on an old gas pump. The number you are aiming at is the placard, not the maximum pressure molded into the sidewall. The sidewall number is a limit for the tire, not the setting for your car.

## Check the placard, not a number we invent here

The placard usually lists a front pressure and a rear pressure, and sometimes a different number when the car is loaded. Use the one that matches how you drive. A compact car and a loaded truck do not share a setting, which is why this shop will not publish one county-wide PSI.

If you just had [new tires](/new-tires) or [used tires](/used-tires) mounted, we set them before you leave, using your placard. Tell us if the placard is gone or if you are running a size that is not what the car was built with. A wrong size is a conversation, not a silent adjustment.

A tire-pressure light is a clue, not a diagnosis. It can mean a slow leak, a nail, a temperature swing, or a sensor that is unhappy. Do not clear the light by adding a random amount of air and forgetting it. If the same tire needs air every few days, you have a leak. That belongs on the [repair](/tire-repair) page: we find it, and we vulcanize it only when the hole is in a safe part of the tread.

## Hurricane season: what to look at before a storm

Hurricane season in Palm Beach County is a reason to look at the tires before the weather, not during the evacuation traffic. This shop is not a shelter and it is not open all night. Before a storm, the useful tire check is ordinary and specific:

- Cold pressure against the door placard, including the spare if you still have one
- Sidewall cracks or bubbles you have been ignoring
- Tread that is already too thin to trust on wet roads
- A slow leak you have been topping off
- The jack and the spare actually present, not a story about a spare that was borrowed

Wet roads punish a bald tire. A spare that is also flat is not a plan. If the spare is a temporary one, know that it is temporary. We can look at it at the shop. We will not promise it is fine from a paragraph.

## When low pressure is actually a puncture

A tire that was correct yesterday and is flat this morning picked up something, or it has a leak at the bead or the valve. Look for a nail in the tread if you can do that without putting yourself in traffic. Then stop driving on it. A short drive on a folding sidewall is how a patchable nail becomes a tire we have to replace.
