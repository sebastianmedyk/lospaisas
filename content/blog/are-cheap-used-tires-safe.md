## What makes a used tire safe or unsafe

A tire does not become dangerous just because someone drove on it before. It becomes dangerous when it has damage you cannot see from the parking lot, too little tread, or the wrong size for the car. These are the things that matter most:

- **Tread depth across the whole tire.** Enough tread is what lets the tire push water away in a Florida downpour. One deep groove is not enough if the shoulder is worn down.
- **The sidewall.** Cuts, bulges, bubbles and cracks on the side of the tire are serious. The sidewall flexes with every turn, and damage there cannot be safely repaired. You can read more in [bulge or cut on the tire sidewall](/en/blog/sidewall-bulge-or-cut-tire).
- **Repairs in the wrong place.** A patch in the tread area is normal. A repair near the shoulder or on the sidewall is a reason to say no.
- **Size and load rating.** A tire that "almost fits" is not the same tire. Size and load rating need to match what your car calls for.
- **Age.** Rubber ages, especially in heat. The DOT code on the sidewall shows when the tire was made. Our guide on [tire age and the DOT date in Florida heat](/en/blog/do-modern-tires-last-only-2-to-4-years) explains how to read it.
- **Uneven wear.** Cupping or one worn edge can mean the tire came off a car with an alignment or suspension problem. That tire may not wear evenly on yours either.

## What we check before mounting, and when we say no

Before a used tire goes on your car at Los Paisas, we look at the whole tire, not just the nice side:

1. **Tread** across the full width, not only the center.
2. **Both sidewalls**, inside and outside, for cuts, bulges and cracks.
3. **Old repairs** and where they are.
4. **Wear pattern** that a new mounting will not fix.
5. **Size and load rating** against your car, not a guess from memory.

You can stand there and look at the tire with us. If a used tire is not a safe way to save, we tell you and show you a different used tire, a repair, or a new option. We would rather lose the sale than mount a tire we would not put on our own car.
