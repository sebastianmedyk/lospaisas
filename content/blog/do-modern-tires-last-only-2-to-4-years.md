How long do tires last in Florida? There are two clocks on every tire: **miles** and **age**. We hear the question a lot at **Los Paisas Tires Shop**, 1114 S Military Trl in West Palm Beach, and the honest answer is this: **good tires still reach 40,000–70,000 miles when you keep the right pressure and alignment, but Florida heat and sun age the rubber, so the DOT date matters too.** What shortens a tire's life in South Florida is usually low pressure, a car out of alignment, and heat and sun, not the calendar alone.

## Quick answer: miles and years

- **Miles:** a good tire can reach about 40–70k miles with correct pressure and alignment. Fast wear usually points to low pressure or alignment, not a bad tire.
- **Years:** many tire and vehicle makers recommend a close inspection once a tire is about 5 years old and replacing it by around 6 to 10 years, even if the tread looks fine. Check your vehicle manual for your maker's advice.
- **Florida:** heat, UV and long hours parked on hot asphalt speed up aging, so check older tires for cracks sooner.

## Do modern tires really last only 2–4 years?

Not as a rule. The feeling is not made up, though. Today's tires are built for lower rolling resistance (better fuel economy) and better grip on wet roads. Softer, grippier rubber that stops well in a Florida downpour can give up a little tread life compared with the very hard tires many drivers remember. That is a trade, not a defect.

So when someone tells us their last set "only lasted two or three years," we look at the tire before we blame the brand. Most of the time the tread itself tells us why.

## What really eats tread: pressure and alignment

Two things decide most of a tire's life, and both are in your control:

- **Pressure.** A tire that runs low flexes more, builds heat, and wears the outer edges. Check it cold, against the sticker on the driver's door jamb, not the number molded on the sidewall. Our guide on [tire pressure in Palm Beach summer](/en/blog/tire-pressure-palm-beach-summer) explains why a hot afternoon reading fools people.
- **Alignment.** A car that pulls, or a steering wheel that sits crooked, scrubs rubber off one shoulder every mile. A curb hit or a pothole is enough to knock it out. See our [wheel alignment service](/en/services/wheel-alignment) and [wheel alignment on Military Trail](/en/blog/wheel-alignment-military-trail-west-palm-beach) for the signs.

Fix those two and a good tire has a fair shot at 40–70k miles. Ignore them and even a premium tire can be finished early.

## How Florida heat and sun affect tire life

Miles are only half of it. Rubber dries out over time, and heat, UV light and long periods parked in the sun speed that up, even when the car barely moves. A tire can still show tread and be too old. Signs of an aging tire:

- Small cracks in the sidewall or between tread blocks
- Rubber that looks dull, gray, or hard
- Bulges or a tread that feels uneven

A bulge or a cut in the sidewall is a stop sign, not a wait-and-see. More in [sidewall bulge or cut](/en/blog/sidewall-bulge-or-cut-tire).

## How to read the DOT date (tire age)

On the sidewall, find the letters **DOT** followed by a code. The **last four digits** are the date: the first two are the **week**, the last two are the **year**. Example: **2321** means the 23rd week of 2021. The full code is sometimes printed only on one side of the tire, so check both sides.

We do not set a fixed number of years as shop law. We read the DOT date together with tread depth and the sidewall, and we tell you what we see. Not sure about your current tires? Stop by and we will read the codes with you.

## How to get the most miles from any tire

- Check cold pressure about once a month and before long trips.
- Get the alignment checked after a hard curb or pothole hit, or if the car pulls.
- Look at the sidewalls now and then for cracks, cuts, or bulges.
- Read the DOT date before you buy, new or used.
- Do not ignore a slow leak; driving low ruins a tire fast.

## FAQ

### How long do tires last in Florida?

It depends on miles and age. Good tires can reach 40–70k miles with correct pressure and alignment. Heat and sun age rubber, so have tires around 5 years old inspected, and many makers advise replacing by around 6 to 10 years. Check your vehicle manual.

### Is it normal for new tires to wear out in 2–4 years?

It depends on how many miles you drive and how the tires are kept. Fast wear usually points to low pressure or alignment, and we can look at the tread pattern to tell which.

### Do softer modern tires wear faster?

Compounds made for lower rolling resistance and better wet grip can wear a bit faster than old hard tires. In exchange you get better grip in the rain.

### What does 1022 mean on my tire?

It was made in the 10th week of 2022. The last four digits of the DOT code are the week and the year.

### My tire has good tread but it is 8 years old. Is it OK?

Have it inspected. At that age, especially in Florida heat, cracks and hidden aging are common, and replacement is often the safer choice.

### Do you check the age of used tires?

Yes. The DOT date is part of our check on every used tire, together with tread and sidewall.

Yes. We compare a checked used tire with a new one in your size, side by side, and you choose.
