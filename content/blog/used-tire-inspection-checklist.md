## Is a used tire safe to buy?

It can be, if it has been checked properly. The risks with used tires are not visible from across the room: worn tread, hidden sidewall damage, old repairs, and age. That is why each tire gets the same checks below before it is offered to you.

## 1. Tread depth

Tread is what grips the road and pushes water away, which matters a lot in Florida rain. We measure the tread across the tire, not just in one spot, and look for uneven wear that can point to an old alignment or pressure problem.

**Check it yourself:** in the US, 2/32" is the legal minimum. Put a penny upside down in the groove; if you can see all of Lincoln's head, the tread is too low.

## 2. Sidewall: cuts, cracks, and bulges

The sidewall cannot be repaired. We look on both sides for:

- **Bulges or bubbles**, a sign the inner structure is damaged
- **Cuts and gouges**, often from curbs or potholes
- **Cracking** from age and sun
- **Plugs or patches near the shoulder or sidewall**, which are not a safe repair

## 3. DOT date code: how old is the tire?

Every tire has a DOT code on the sidewall. The **last four digits** are the week and year it was made. For example, **2421** means the 24th week of 2021. Rubber ages even when the tread looks good, and Florida heat speeds that up, so the age is part of the decision. More on this in [how to read the DOT date](/en/blog/tire-age-dot-date-florida-heat).

## 4. Inside and bead

We also check the inside of the tire and the bead (the edge that seals to the wheel) for signs it was driven flat or damaged during an old mount.
