## The cheapest tire is the one you already have

A nail or screw in the tread does not always mean a new tire. Many punctures in the tread area can be repaired properly, and a repaired tire can keep doing its job. That costs far less than a replacement and keeps the tires on the axle matched.

The key is knowing when a repair is safe. A repair that should not have been done is not cheap. It is a risk you take every time you get on the highway.

## Patch, plug, vulcanization: when it's safe

The three words mean slightly different things:

- **Plug:** a quick seal pushed into the puncture from the outside.
- **Patch:** a repair applied from the inside of the tire after it is removed from the wheel.
- **Vulcanization:** a repair that bonds the material with heat, used for some kinds of damage.

Which one is right depends on the damage. What matters most for safety is **where** the damage is:

- **Tread area (the part that touches the road):** small punctures here are usually repairable.
- **Shoulder (the edge between tread and sidewall):** repairs here are risky and often not recommended.
- **Sidewall:** cuts, bulges and punctures on the side of the tire **cannot be safely repaired**. The sidewall flexes with every turn, and a repair there will not hold. Read more in [bulge or cut on the tire sidewall](/en/blog/sidewall-bulge-or-cut-tire).

We also check the inside of the tire. If the tire was driven flat, the inside can be damaged even when the outside looks fine, and then a repair is not safe either.

If you are already driving on a repaired tire and wondering how long it can last, see [driving on a tire patch or plug](/en/blog/driving-on-a-tire-patch-or-plug).
