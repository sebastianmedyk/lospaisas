## Why the cheapest sticker price isn't always the cheapest tire

A tire is only one part of the bill. To drive away, the tire has to be mounted on the wheel and balanced so the car does not shake at speed. Some prices you see online or on a sign cover the tire alone. Mounting, balancing, valve stems, disposal of the old tire or a "shop fee" can show up later, at the counter, when you already have the car in the bay and little room to say no.

That is why the honest way to compare is the **out-the-door price**: what you pay in total for one tire, mounted and balanced, on your car. A tire that looks $10 cheaper can end up costing more once the extras are added. And a cheap tire that is the wrong size, or a used tire with a damaged sidewall, is not cheap at all if you are back in a shop next week.

There is also the question of **warranty**. A new tire with a warranty and a used tire without one are not the same product, even when the prices look close. Knowing what you are buying is part of knowing the price.

## 5 questions to ask any tire shop before you pay

You can use this list at any shop, including ours. Ask before anyone lifts the car:

1. **Is mounting included in the tire price?** If not, how much is it per tire?
2. **Is balancing included?** An unbalanced tire can shake at highway speed and wear unevenly.
3. **Is there any fee added at the end?** Ask the question directly so there is no surprise.
4. **Is there a warranty, and for how long?** For new tires, ask the time and the mileage. For used tires, ask whether there is any warranty at all.
5. **Are you open when I need you?** If you can only come on a Sunday, a lower price at a shop that is closed that day does not help.

Write down the answers next to the price. Then compare totals, not headlines. This is the simplest answer to the question "what is the cheapest place to get tires mounted and balanced": the place where the total for the job, done right, is lowest.

## Used, new or repair: the cheapest safe option for your car

The cheapest option depends on what is wrong with the tire and how you drive.

**Repair first, if it is safe.** If a nail went into the tread area, a patch or vulcanization may save the tire you already own. That is often the cheapest fix of all. A patch usually takes about 15 to 20 minutes, depending on how busy the shop is. A cut or bulge on the sidewall cannot be safely repaired, and we will tell you so. See [tire repair](/en/services/tire-repair).

**A checked used tire** makes sense when you need to replace one tire, you want to match the tread of the others, or your budget is tight this month. We look at the tread, the sidewalls and the size before mounting, and if a used tire is the wrong way to save, we say so. Our [used tires page](/en/services/used-tires) explains what we check.

**A new tire** makes more sense when you drive long distances, when you need a full set, or when you want the warranty. With 4 new tires you also get free alignment, which protects the new set from uneven wear.
