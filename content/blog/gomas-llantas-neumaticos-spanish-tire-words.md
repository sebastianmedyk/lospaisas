## One tire, several words

Spanish is spoken in many countries, and each one has its own everyday word for a tire. None of them is wrong. A quick, friendly overview:

- **Llanta** is very common in Mexico, Colombia and much of Central America, and it is the word most tire shops in the US use on Spanish signs. In some places "llanta" can also mean the rim, so it helps to point at what you mean.
- **Goma** is the word many drivers from Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic and Cuba use day to day. If you hear "se me ponchó la goma", it means a flat tire.
- **Neumático** is the more formal or technical word. You see it in manuals and in Spain.
- **Caucho** is common in Venezuela.
- **Rin** usually means the wheel or rim, the metal part the tire sits on.

You do not need the "right" word. Show us the tire, send a photo by WhatsApp, or read us the size printed on the sidewall, and we will take it from there.

## A few other tire words that come up

When drivers explain what is happening to their car in Spanish, these words come up often:

- **Ponchada / ponchadura**: a flat tire or a puncture.
- **Parche** and **tapón**: a patch and a plug, two kinds of repair.
- **Balanceo**: wheel balancing, so the car does not shake.
- **Alineación**: wheel alignment.
- **Piso** or **banda**: the tread, the part that touches the road.
- **Costado**: the sidewall.
- **Medida**: the tire size, such as 205/55R16.
