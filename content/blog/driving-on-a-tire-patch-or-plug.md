## A patch is not a promise you can ignore the tire

People drive on a plugged tire because the car still feels round. That can be true for a while and still be a bad bet. A plug pushed in from the outside does not let anyone see the inside of the tire. The puncture can be angled. The inner liner can be torn bigger than the hole you see. A previous plug can be the thing that is leaking now.

If we repair a tire, we take it off the wheel when we need to see that inner liner. Vulcanization seals the injury from the inside. That is the repair we are willing to talk about as something you can drive on. A rope plug in a parking lot is not the same sentence. We will not tell you a parking-lot plug is "probably fine" over the phone. Bring the car in, or call if you cannot.

After a proper repair, you still watch the tire. A repair does not add tread. It does not fix a tire that was already bald. If the pressure drops again, the repair is not a success and the tire should not stay in service while you "see how the week goes."

## Tread punctures versus sidewall damage

The tread is where a small nail can be repairable. The sidewall is not. A cut, a bubble, or a hole in the sidewall is a replacement, [used](/services/used-tires) or [new](/services/new-tires), after we look at what we have in your size. The shoulder, where the tread curves into the sidewall, is the area we are strict about. A hole that looks "almost in the tread" can be too far over to seal safely. We would rather lose the repair than send you out on it.

Driving on a flat, or on a tire that is low enough to fold the sidewall, can turn a repairable nail into a tire that is finished. The rim cuts the inner liner. You feel it as a thump, or you do not feel it until the tire lets go. If the tire is already down, do not keep rolling "just to Military Trl" unless you are truly a few yards away and even then you may be ruining it. Use a spare if you have one.

## If the tire is losing air right now

Pull off the road somewhere safe. Do not sit in a live lane. If you have a spare and you know how to use it, that is the way to get to the shop. If you do not, call **+1 561-429-4041** and say where you are. If you are inside 10 miles and we can work there, we will tell you. Do not keep adding air at a gas station all week. A tire that needs air every morning has a leak. The nail you can see is not always the only leak. Driving it "topped off" is how people show up with a destroyed sidewall and a repair that is no longer possible.

## After a repair, what we still check

A repaired tire should hold air. We check that before you leave. You should not see the sidewall folding, and you should not hear a hiss. If the tread was already uneven, the repair did not straighten the car. A pull is an [alignment](/services/wheel-alignment) question for another part of the same visit, not a sign that the patch "didn't take."

Set the cold pressure from the placard on your door, not from a number you remember from a different car. Heat will raise it after you drive. That is normal. A repair does not change the placard.

## Questions about driving on a repair

### Can I drive on a tire with the nail still in it?

Only as far as you must to get somewhere safe, and only if it is not already going flat. A nail that is "holding" is still a leak waiting. Call and come in the same day if you can. Hours run until 7:00 PM, including Sunday.

### Is a plug from a kit enough?

We do not treat an outside plug as a finished repair. Vulcanization means the tire comes off the wheel and the injury is sealed from the inside. If that cannot be done safely, the tire is replaced.

### Will you patch it so I can "just get home" out of state?

No. We do not install a temporary patch we already know is in the wrong part of the tire. If it is not repairable, it is not repairable for a long drive either.
